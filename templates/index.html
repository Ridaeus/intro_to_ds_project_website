Unfamiliar indoor spaces, navigation, and orientation

Who can deny that visiting new places is frustrating at best, wholly disorienting at worst. Not knowing where you stand or where to go. Although tactile maps and various software developed for item and text detection can be helpful in their own ways, they come short of providing spacial awareness, and a sense of position and orientation on the go. Tactile maps are placed sparingly, if present at all. AI recognition software are great at telling you what may be present nearby, reading you product descriptions, and telling you names of people, but they are not designed to help you walk through buildings and rooms to your destination.

Our solution is combining depth perception with spacial audio

This auditory navigation project uses an infrared depth camera to detect important landmarks of indoor spaces, such as doors and railings, and relays their position to you via sound marks that change depending on the object's proximity and relative position. In this way, it can build an environment around you by sound, giving you a sense of position and orientation.

Imagine walking into a new library building to meet a friend. There are no maps present in the entrance, but you came prepared because you had someone draw the map for you beforehand. You know that after leaving the elevator you walk straight, passing through one door; then you take the first right turn and keep walking straight past three meeting cubicles and reach the open area. Your will meet your friends there. This auditory navigation will be there to confirm that you are indeed walking towards a door, that you indeed took the correct right turn because you can *hear* that you just past the three consecutive doors of the three cubicles. While waiting for your friend , if you need reassurance about which way you are facing, you can anchor yourself in relation to these cubicle doors.

While this project is yet in its inception, we see its potential to become an easily accessible tool through use of mobile devices and consumer headphones.
